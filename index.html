          05 · Pricing playbook
          
      
          Ten moves that pay for themselves.
          
      
          Tactics that hold up in real negotiations, with the exact words to use where words matter.
          
    
          
    
          
      
          
        
          01
          
        
          Say the value out loud before you say the price.
          
        
          Before any number, restate the outcome in the client's own words and money: "You mentioned abandoned carts cost you around $40k a month." Your fee is now judged against $40k, not against another freelancer's hourly rate.
          
        
          Price always lands in whatever frame was set right before it. Set it yourself.
          
      
          
      
          
        
          02
          
        
          Design three tiers from the middle out.
          
        
          Write the package you actually want to sell first. Strip it down for Good, so it solves the problem but leaves obvious value on the table. Load Best with speed, access, and ongoing support, priced at 1.6 to 2.5 times the middle.
          
        
          Buyers avoid extremes. The top tier exists to make the middle feel sensible, and now and then someone buys it.
          
      
          
      
          
        
          03
          
        
          Never discount. Trade scope.
          
        
          A discount tells the client your first number was padded, and they will push on every future quote. When they ask for a lower price, move deliverables instead.
          
        
          Say this"I can work with that budget. To get there, we'd drop the usability testing and go to one revision round. Which of those matters less to you?"
          
      
          
      
          
        
          04
          
        
          Diagnose "that's too expensive" before you defend it.
          
        
          The objection means one of three things, and each has a different fix. Ask which one it is.
          
        
          Say this"Thanks for being direct. Is that compared to another quote, compared to the budget you had in mind, or compared to what you expect this to return?"
          
        
          Another quote: explain the difference in risk and outcome. Budget: offer the Good tier or split into phases. Return: rebuild the value math together, or part as friends.
          
      
          
      
          
        
          05
          
        
          Raise rates on existing clients every year, with 60 days notice.
          
        
          Clients rarely leave over a 10 to 15% increase delivered calmly and early. They leave over surprises. Put it in writing, keep it short, and do not apologize.
          
        
          Send this"Hi Sam, a quick note for your planning: from March 1, my rate for new work moves to $165 per hour. Anything we have already scoped stays at the current rate. Thank you for a great year. I'm looking forward to what we build next."
          
      
          
      
          
        
          06
          
        
          Retainers sell priority, not a bucket of hours.
          
        
          Price a retainer on guaranteed availability and response time. Set a three month minimum, and let unused time expire at month end, or roll over one month at most.
          
        
          A 5 to 10% discount against project rates is a fair trade for predictable income. Hours that roll over forever turn you into the client's bank.
          
      
          
      
          
        
          07
          
        
          Charge for discovery.
          
        
          Sell a paid diagnostic, usually 10 to 15% of the likely project, that ends in a roadmap the client owns. It filters out tire kickers, pays you for the hardest thinking, and turns your proposal into an informed recommendation instead of a guess.
          
        
          Credit the fee toward the project if they sign within 30 days. Most do.
          
      
          
      
          
        
          08
          
        
          Use precise numbers when you expect a counter.
          
        
          Negotiation research consistently finds that precise anchors like 18,400 draw smaller counteroffers than round ones like 18,000, because they read as calculated rather than guessed.
          
        
          Use round numbers for premium tiers where you want to signal confidence. Use precise ones when you know procurement will push.
          
      
          
      
          
        
          09
          
        
          Write rush and change fees into the contract up front.
          
        
          A fee the client agreed to in advance is a policy. A fee you invent mid project is an argument. Spell it out: changes are quoted as change orders, work needed in under five business days is billed at 1.5x, weekend work at 2x.
          
        
          You will rarely enforce it. Its job is to make "can you just..." expensive enough to think about.
          
      
          
      
          
        
          10
          
        
          Treat the deposit as a filter, not just cash flow.
          
        
          Ask for 50% to start and the rest on milestones, never all on completion. Work begins when the deposit clears, which protects your calendar from clients who are "almost ready".
          
        
          A client who balks at a deposit is telling you exactly how invoicing will go.
