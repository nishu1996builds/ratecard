## Thread 1: The utilization trap (freelance line)

**1/**
Most freelance rate calculators assume you bill every hour you work.

You won't. And that one assumption can make your rate 2.5x too low.

Here's the math:

**2/**
Example inputs: $90k take-home, 30% tax, $1,200/mo business costs, 6 weeks off, 40-hour weeks, 10% margin.

Minimum hourly rate needed:
100% billable: ~$85
60% billable: ~$142
40% billable: ~$214

Same life. Same costs.

**3/**
Why you won't bill 100%:

Sales calls. Proposals. Invoicing. Admin. Chasing payments. Gaps between projects.

All real work. None of it billable.

50% to 65% is a realistic planning range for most solo businesses.

**4/**
What to do with this:

1. Work out your floor using honest utilization.
2. Never quote the floor. Quote 25%+ above it.
3. Use that room to trade scope, not to discount.

**5/**
A slow quarter hurts twice: less revenue, and the same overhead spread across fewer billed hours.

Price for the year you'll actually have, not the one on paper.

## Thread 2: Tailoring a resume in 15 minutes (job search line)

Before posting: match the steps below to the wording in your actual checklist.

**1/**
You don't need to rewrite your resume for every job.

You need 15 minutes and a routine. Here's one:

**2/**
Min 0 to 3: Read the posting twice. Mark the skills and tools that show up more than once, and the outcomes they care about.

**3/**
Min 3 to 6: Compare to your resume.

Which terms are missing? Which do you have, but in different words?

**4/**
Min 6 to 12: Edit, don't rewrite.

Use their wording where it's honestly true for you. Move the most relevant bullets to the top of each role. Rewrite the first line of your summary.

**5/**
Min 12 to 15: Check the basics.

Single column. Standard headings. No tables or text boxes. Every claim is something you could explain in an interview.

**6/**
The rule that keeps it honest: only use a keyword if you've done the thing.

Tailoring is translation, not invention.
